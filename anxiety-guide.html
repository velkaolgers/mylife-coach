Why I created it

For years, I've helped people navigate anxiety, stress, overthinking, and the pressures of demanding lives. And one thing I've learned is that anxiety doesn't always show up in the ways we expect. Sometimes, it appears in the most ordinary moments, making things that seem so simple to others feel surprisingly difficult.

I know that feeling well. As a young adult, I struggled with anxiety myself. I remember how something as ordinary as learning to drive could suddenly feel overwhelming. The anticipation, the nervousness, the endless thoughts about what might go wrong. And the more I wanted to feel calm and confident, the harder it sometimes seemed.

Looking back, I wish I'd understood then what I know today: trying harder to feel calm isn't always what helps us find it.

That experience has stayed with me throughout my work as a coach and hypnotherapist. It has shaped the way I understand anxiety, not just as something I've studied and helped others navigate, but as something I've experienced myself.

Over the years, I've explored many approaches to helping the mind settle, and there are a few techniques I find myself returning to again and again. Simple, gentle practices that can help us shift our attention, ease the intensity of anxious thoughts, and create a little more space to breathe.

And that's what I'd love to share with you here: one of my favourite techniques, something I often incorporate into my work and that you can learn to use yourself.

I'm sharing it for free because I know how much difference even one small, helpful tool can make when anxiety starts to take over.
